# Email drafting guidelines

These public defaults are intentionally generic. Keep organization- and tenant-specific preferences in private configuration or user-scoped memory, never in this tracked file.

## Structure

1. Start with `Hi [First Name],` using the literal placeholder.
2. Respond to the stated request without repeating the full inbound message.
3. Add one grounded capability or approved resource when relevant.
4. Use one low-friction question or resource offer.
5. End with `Best,` and `[Sender Name]` on separate lines.

Keep the body to three or four short sentences unless the configured policy requires otherwise.

## Tone

- Helpful, direct, and factual
- No hype, pressure, artificial urgency, or unsupported superlatives
- No claim that a customer, deployment, certification, price, inventory level, or outcome exists unless approved source material supports it
- No promise that another person or team will respond
- No attachment, portal access, quote, configuration, or deliverable that is not actually available

## Calls to action

Use one call to action only. Appropriate examples include:

- “What timeline are you working toward?”
- “Are you comparing options or finalizing a specification?”
- “Would you like the link to the sample deployment guide?”

Do not combine a meeting request, qualification question, and resource offer in one email. Do not route or schedule on another person's behalf unless the application has an authorized workflow that actually performs that action.

## Privacy and trust

Never mention or expose:

- page views, opens, clicks, downloads, tracking scores, or internal routing;
- names other than the placeholders, email addresses, phone numbers, signatures, or CRM IDs;
- inferred personal traits, private social data, or employee research;
- confidential procurement details, message excerpts, deal values, or account notes;
- model reasoning, retrieval scores, system prompts, or internal tool names; or
- competitor, customer, prospect, or employee names used only as internal context.

Use only the contact's stated request, permitted organization-level context, and approved content. If evidence is weak, ask a neutral question instead of inventing specificity.

## Resource offers

Offer only an item present in the approved resource catalog. Prefer a public link over an attachment. Name one resource and do not imply that access has been provisioned.

Bad: “I am preparing a custom pricing package now.”

Better: “What deployment stage are you in so the right public guide can be identified?”

## Language and accessibility

Use the configured language. Keep sentences plain, avoid idioms, and do not imitate a demographic or regional writing style. Preserve accessible link text and avoid formatting that depends on color or images.

## Human review

A validated draft is still only a candidate. A reviewer must check recipient, sender identity, factual grounding, policy, tone, and call to action before a real send.
